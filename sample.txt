The gender related concepts include: Gender roles, gender bias, gender equity and equality,
gender blind, gender gap, gender stereotyping.
Gender Roles: These are the expectations and norms that a society has for individuals based
on their perceived gender. Traditional gender roles often dictate that men should be assertive,
strong, independent, intelligent, rational, brave, ambitious, competitive and dominant, while
women should be dependent, nurturing, passive, timid, weak, cooperative, sensitive,
receptive, emotional, and supportive. These roles can vary significantly across different
cultures and historical periods.
Gender Equality: This concept involves the pursuit of equal rights, responsibilities, and
opportunities for people of all genders. It aims to eliminate gender-based discrimination and
promote fairness and justice in all areas of life, from education and employment to health and
politics. It refers to different behaviours, aspirations and needs of men and women that are
considered, valued and favoured equally. It entails the concept that all humans, both men and
women are free to develop their personal abilities and make choices without the limitations
set by stereotypes, rigid gender roles, or prejudices. It does not mean that men and women
have to become the same, but their rights, responsibilities and opportunities will not depend
on whether they are born male or female.
Gender Equity: This refers to the fair treatment of individuals regardless of their gender,
ensuring that everyone has equal opportunities and access to resources. Unlike gender
equality, which focuses on providing the same resources and opportunities to all genders,
gender equity involves recognizing the different needs and challenges faced by different

genders and addressing these disparities through targeted measures. The key aspects of
gender equity include:
Equal Access: Ensuring that all genders have equal access to education, healthcare,
employment, and political participation.
Fair Treatment: Creating environments where individuals are treated fairly and respectfully,
free from gender-based discrimination and harassment.
Supportive Policies: Implementing policies that support work-life balance, such as parental
leave and flexible working hours, to accommodate the diverse needs of all genders.
Removing Barriers: Identifying, and dismantling systemic barriers that prevent individuals
from achieving their full potential based on gender.
Empowerment: Encouraging and enabling individuals of all genders to participate fully in all
aspects of life, fostering leadership and decision-making opportunities.
Gender equity is crucial for creating a just and inclusive society where everyone can thrive,
irrespective of gender. It requires ongoing effort and commitment to addressing inequalities
and promoting fairness in all spheres of life.
Gender Blind: The failure to recognize the roles and responsibilities of men, and women
given to them in specific social, cultural, economic, and political contexts and backgrounds.
Projects, programmes, policies and attitudes which are gender blind do not take into account
these different roles and diverse needs, maintain status quo, and will not help transform the
unequal structure of gender relations.
Gender Bias: This refers to the preferential treatment or prejudice against individuals based
on their gender, often resulting in unfair advantages or disadvantages. This bias can manifest
in various areas, including the workplace, education, healthcare, and media, perpetuating
stereotypes and reinforcing gender inequalities. Addressing gender bias requires awareness,
education, and systemic changes to create a more equitable and inclusive society where all
individuals have the opportunity to thrive, free from prejudice and discrimination.
Gender gap: This refers to the differences between men and women in levels of achievement
or access. This could for example be access to education or health care and treatment services
or differentials in wages paid to women and men. These differentials may result from
customary practices, religious biases, social assumption, myths or taboos, among others.
Gender Stereotyping: This refers to assigning of roles, tasks and responsibilities to a
particular gender on the basis of preconceived prejudices. Gender stereotyping is nothing but
reinforcing feminine and masculine qualities and attributes again and again. Gender
stereotypes are beliefs which prescribe what men and women should and what they can do. It
can be changed over period of time, but changes are very slow.

1.3 Exploring Attitudes towards Gender
This dimension focuses on how extent to which a society stress achievement or
nurture. Masculinity is seen to be the trait which emphasizes ambition, acquisition of wealth,
and differentiated gender roles. Femininity is seen to be the trait which stresses caring and
nurturing behaviours, sexuality equality, environmental awareness, and more fluid gender
roles. The biological differences between men and women get translated into social terms and
descriptions. The biological differences get heightened through social descriptions of
masculinity and femininity.
When people interact, their view of themselves, including their identity and their rights and
possibilities, comes up against the way they are perceived by other people, and the way that
others behave towards them. Attitudes towards Gender (also referred to as sex role attitudes)
have been defined as "an individual's beliefs about appropriate behaviour for men and
women". One's gender role attitudes are likely to indicate her or his adherence to socially
accepted and culturally based norms that dictate behavioural standards in cross - sex
relationships. Gender role attitudes indicate the degree to which a person accepts or rejects
socially accepted and culturally based norms that dictate appropriate behaviour that is gender
specific.
Social Attitudes surveys have included attitudinal questions asking about the roles of men
and women within the family, in particular around providing an income from work versus
playing a caring role in the home. Examples of gender role attitudes include such statements
as "men should open the door for women," "fathers and mothers should participate equally in
nurturing and caring for their children," "men are primarily responsible for earning money
and providing for their family," and "women should adopt their husband's surname upon
marriage." Gender role attitudes and ideas about traits or tasks that are specific or inherent to
a particular gender encourage us to stereotype or pigeonhole individuals according to their
gender. Obviously, this practice has negative implications for both males and females.
